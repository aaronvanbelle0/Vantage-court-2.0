Why base preparation decides everything

Most court failures are not surface failures. They start underneath, where poor drainage or an inadequately compacted sub-base lets water move and the slab shift. In Northwest Washington that is the dominant risk: rainfall is heavy, the water table is high in a lot of the valley, and freeze-thaw cycling works on any crack that has already formed.

That is why the first phase of every project is a survey rather than a quote — checking slope, identifying low spots, and working out where water goes. A court built on a good base and surfaced adequately will outlast a court built on a poor base and surfaced immaculately.

Choosing between resurfacing and rebuilding

A court that has faded, lost its texture, or developed surface cracking is usually a resurfacing candidate, and resurfacing costs a fraction of a new build. A court where the slab itself has moved — wide structural cracks, sections at different heights, persistent standing water that grading cannot fix — is a different conversation.

We will tell you which one you are looking at, including when the honest answer is that resurfacing would be money spent on a surface that will crack again within a couple of seasons. Sometimes the answer is repair now and rebuild in five years, and that is a legitimate plan.

Frequently asked questions

What sports do you build courts for?

Pickleball, tennis, and basketball on acrylic hard-court systems, plus sand volleyball. Multi-sport courts with blended, color-coded lines let a single footprint carry more than one game.

Do you work on commercial and public projects?

Yes. We handle prevailing wage and public works projects as well as residential courts, and we work with specified systems including Plexipave where a project calls for a particular manufacturer.

Are estimates free?

Yes. On-site estimates are free anywhere in our service area, and they are how we price accurately — drainage, access, and existing surface condition are not things anyone can judge from photographs.

Are you licensed and insured?

Yes, licensed and insured in Washington. We are based in Lynden and the crew is local.

What areas do you serve?

Whatcom, Skagit, Snohomish, Island, and San Juan Counties, including Bellingham, Lynden, Ferndale, Blaine, Mount Vernon, Burlington, Anacortes, Oak Harbor, Stanwood, Marysville, and Friday Harbor.

How long does a typical project take?

Resurfacing generally runs several days to about a week on site. New construction runs two to three weeks for pickleball and basketball courts and three to five weeks for full-size tennis. Weather drives most of the variance in both cases.
