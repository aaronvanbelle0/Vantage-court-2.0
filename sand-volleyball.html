How We Build

What goes into a sand court.

A sand volleyball court is mostly about what's underneath the sand. Drainage is what separates a court that plays well in April from one that's a swamp all winter.

1

Excavation & Drainage

We dig out the court to depth, install a perforated drain field and gravel base so water moves through the sand instead of sitting on top of it.

2

Edging & Containment

Timber, composite, aluminum, or concrete edging keeps sand in the court and grass or gravel out. We pick the material based on budget and traffic.

3

Sand Fill

Washed, screened volleyball-grade sand — not construction sand or beach sand, which compact hard or hold too much moisture. Filled and leveled to spec depth.

4

Posts, Net & Lines

Sleeve-mounted steel posts, a regulation net system, and boundary lines set with in-ground anchors or surface tape depending on your preference.

Built for Northwest Weather

Sand that doesn't turn to mud.

Sand courts fail in this climate for one reason more than any other: bad drainage. We treat it as the most important part of the build, not an afterthought.

Real drainage, not just gravel

Perforated pipe tied into a proper outlet, not just a rock layer and hope. Courts drain within hours of heavy rain instead of staying soggy for days.

Volleyball-grade sand only

Washed and screened to spec — soft enough to play safely on, coarse enough to drain instead of compacting into a hard, muddy layer.

Edging that actually holds

Properly anchored borders keep sand from migrating into the lawn and keep grass and weeds from creeping into the court.

Post systems that don't shift

Sleeve-mounted posts set in concrete footings so net tension stays consistent season after season, even as the sand settles.

FAQ

Common questions.

What kind of sand do you use? +

Washed, screened volleyball-grade sand. It's specified for grain size and roundness so it's soft for diving but drains well — regular play sand, beach sand, or construction sand aren't the same thing and compact or hold water.

Will the court hold up through winter? +

With proper drainage underneath, yes. That's the whole reason we treat drainage as the most important part of the build in this climate rather than an add-on.

How much sand does a court need? +

A backyard court runs about 12" of sand depth; regulation courts typically use 16"-18". We calculate exact volume once we confirm your court dimensions on site.

Do you handle the net and post system too? +

Yes — sleeve-mounted posts set in concrete footings, a regulation net, and boundary lines are all part of the build.

Do you top off sand on an existing court? +

Yes, if the base and drainage underneath are sound we can add or replace sand without rebuilding the whole court. If drainage is the problem, we'll tell you honestly rather than just adding sand on top of a bad base.

What areas do you serve? +

Whatcom, Skagit, Snohomish, Island, and San Juan Counties. If you're nearby and not sure, give us a call.
