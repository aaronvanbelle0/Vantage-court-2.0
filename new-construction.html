How We Build

What goes into a court.

A court is more than what you see on top. The work underneath — base prep and drainage — is what determines how long it lasts.

Step 1

Site Prep & Grading

We start with a proper survey — checking sun direction, drainage patterns, and how the court fits the lot. Then we excavate, install drainage if needed, and grade the sub-base.

  • ✓ Site survey and layout
  • ✓ Excavation and grading
  • ✓ Drainage installation
  • ✓ Compacted aggregate sub-base

Step 2

Concrete or Asphalt Base

For Pacific Northwest weather, we usually recommend post-tensioned concrete — it resists cracking from freeze-thaw cycles. Asphalt is a budget option that works but needs more upkeep over time.

  • ✓ Post-tensioned concrete (recommended)
  • ✓ Standard reinforced concrete
  • ✓ Asphalt option for budget builds
  • ✓ Proper slope for drainage (1% standard)

Step 3

Acrylic Surface Coating

Multiple layers of acrylic surface — typically a resurfacer coat, two color coats, and line paint. We use proven manufacturers like California Sports Surfaces and Plexipave.

  • ✓ Acrylic resurfacer base coat
  • ✓ Two color finish coats
  • ✓ Cushioned option for joint comfort
  • ✓ 10+ color choices available

Step 4

Lines, Nets & Finish

Lines are pulled to spec — USTA, USA Pickleball, or whatever standard you're using. Net posts, nets, fencing, and any extras like windscreens or lighting are the final step before we hand it over.

  • ✓ Precision line striping
  • ✓ Net post installation
  • ✓ Fencing and gates
  • ✓ Windscreens, lighting (optional)

Common questions about building a court

How much does it cost to build a pickleball court in Washington?

A single backyard pickleball court on an easy site runs about $37,000 to $54,000 before fencing and lighting. A full-size tennis court runs about $78,000 to $115,000, and a residential basketball half-court about $42,000 to $62,000. Sloped or hard-to-reach sites add 15 to 35 percent. Those are general ranges — every site is different, so we give a real number after we visit.

How long does it take to build a sport court?

Roughly two to three weeks of build time for pickleball and basketball courts, and three to five weeks for a full-size tennis court. Concrete cure time and weather drive most of the variation, and acrylic surfacing needs dry conditions to go down properly.

Should I use concrete or asphalt for the base?

For Pacific Northwest weather we usually recommend post-tensioned concrete, because it resists the cracking that freeze-thaw cycles cause. Asphalt is a budget option that works, but it needs more upkeep over time and is more likely to need crack repair sooner.

How much space do I need for a court?

A pickleball court has a 20 by 44 foot playing area, with 30 by 60 feet recommended overall to allow for run-off and fencing. Full-size tennis is 60 by 120 feet overall. A full basketball court is 50 by 94 feet; most backyards go half-court at 47 by 50 feet.

Do I need a permit to build a court?

It depends on your jurisdiction and what the project involves. Grading, drainage work, fencing height, and lighting are the elements most likely to trigger a permit requirement. We flag what your site is likely to need during the estimate, and permit costs are additional to the project total.
