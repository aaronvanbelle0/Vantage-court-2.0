What Moves the Number

The five biggest cost drivers.

Two courts with identical lines can differ by tens of thousands of dollars. This is why.

1

The site

Slope, soil, access

A level lawn is the cheap case. Sloped lots need cut, fill, and sometimes retaining walls; moderate site prep adds about 15% and difficult sites about 35%. Whether an excavator and concrete truck can reach the pad matters as much as the slope.

2

The base

Asphalt vs. concrete

Asphalt is about 20% cheaper upfront than post-tensioned concrete but cracks sooner in freeze-thaw. Reinforced concrete sits in between and is what most backyard courts are built on.

3

Fencing

From ~$13K

Perimeter fencing and gates are often the single biggest add-on, and fence height is a common permit trigger.

4

Lighting

From ~$18K

LED court lighting extends play into the evening, but it brings electrical work and is the add-on most likely to need a permit.

5

Number of courts

~85% each after the first

Shared site work and a shared fence make each additional court on the same slab cheaper than the first.

+

Smaller extras

Windscreens, cushion, hoops

Windscreens run about $1.5K, a cushioned surface about $6K, and an in-ground basketball hoop about $4.5K installed.

Saving Money

Ways to keep the cost down.

Without building something you will regret in five years.

Go multi-sport

One slab with blended pickleball and basketball lines costs far less than two separate courts.

Phase the extras

Pour the slab and surface now; add lighting or windscreens later. Plan conduit before the pour so lighting is easy to add.

Book the survey early

Surfacing season here runs roughly late spring through early fall. Getting on the schedule early avoids a rushed job or a year's delay.

Sport court cost questions

How much does a sport court cost per square foot?

Measured across the full pad including run-off, a new concrete court in Northwest Washington runs roughly $20 to $30 per square foot for pickleball, $18 to $26 for a basketball half-court, and $11 to $16 for a full tennis court, before fencing and lighting. Smaller courts cost more per square foot because fixed costs are spread over less area.

How much does court resurfacing cost per square foot?

Roughly $2.20 to $3.00 per square foot for a tennis court, $3.00 to $4.00 for a basketball half-court, and $4.70 to $6.40 for a pickleball court, including wash, crack repair, color coats, and lines on a sound slab.

How much does a pickleball court cost in Washington?

About $37,000 to $54,000 for a single backyard court on an easy, level site with a concrete base, before fencing and lighting. Asphalt starts around $32,000. Fencing, lighting, slopes, and access push the total higher.

How much does a tennis court cost?

About $78,000 to $115,000 for a full-size court on an easy site with a concrete base, before fencing and lighting. Asphalt starts around $68,000. Difficult sites can add about a third.

How much does a backyard basketball court cost?

About $42,000 to $62,000 for a half-court with an acrylic surface on a concrete slab, and about $66,000 to $97,000 for a full court, before hoops, fencing, and lighting.

How much does it cost to resurface a court?

Roughly $8,500 to $11,500 for a pickleball court, $16,000 to $22,000 for a tennis court, and $7,000 to $9,500 for a basketball half-court, assuming the slab is structurally sound.

Why do online prices look lower than yours?

National averages often leave out site work, assume dry-climate bases, or quote surfacing only. In Northwest Washington, drainage and a base that survives freeze-thaw are where courts succeed or fail, and they are in our numbers.
