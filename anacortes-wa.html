Sport court construction in Anacortes.

Anacortes is on Fidalgo Island, and island ground behaves differently from valley ground. Rock, salt air, and wind exposure all show up in how a court gets built here.

Thin soil over bedrock

Much of Fidalgo Island is shallow soil over rock. That cuts both ways. Bedrock is excellent bearing - a slab on competent rock is not going to settle - but hitting rock during excavation changes the job. Breaking or hammering rock to reach grade costs considerably more than moving dirt, and it is not always predictable from the surface.

We probe during the site visit and price with that in mind rather than quoting a dirt-excavation number and revising it once the excavator is on site. Where rock is shallow, sometimes the better answer is to raise the pad rather than cut into the rock.

Salt air and wind

Marine air is hard on metal. Fence framework, net posts, gate hardware, and hoop systems corrode faster within sight of the water than they do inland, so galvanized and coated hardware is worth specifying here rather than treating as an upgrade.

Exposed sites also catch real wind. That matters for play, and it matters for fence design. Windscreens make an exposed court usable on more days of the year, but they also load the fence, so the fence has to be built for it.

Permitting in Anacortes

City of Anacortes handles permitting inside the city; Skagit County covers the rest of Fidalgo Island. Shoreline designations apply to waterfront and near-shore properties and can add review steps worth identifying early.

Court questions in Anacortes

Does hitting rock change the cost of a court in Anacortes?

It can, significantly. Rock excavation is slower and requires different equipment than moving soil. We probe during the site visit to understand what is under the pad area, and where rock sits shallow, building the pad up rather than cutting down is sometimes the cheaper route.

Will salt air damage the fencing and hardware?

It will corrode standard hardware faster than an inland site would. Galvanized and coated components are worth specifying on Fidalgo Island. It is a modest addition to the project cost and it materially extends the life of everything metal on the court.

Is wind a problem for a court here?

On exposed sites, yes - enough to affect play on pickleball and tennis courts. Windscreens on the fence help considerably. They also add wind load to the fence, so the fence needs to be specified for it rather than screens being added to a standard fence later.

Do you charge extra to work in Anacortes?

Anacortes is connected by road, so there is no ferry or barge premium the way there is on the San Juans. Mobilization is a normal drive from Lynden. On-site estimates are free.
